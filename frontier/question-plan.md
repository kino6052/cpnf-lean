# Plan the attack. Be merciless and specific.

You are the frontier. Three routes are now dead, each with a proved mechanism, not a failure
report: sieves (parity barrier), the binary circle method (no spare factor at a fixed shift),
and affine-linear-form statistics (your gate construction: a set with the full tuple asymptotics
for all non-proportional systems, yet zero twin pairs). You proved that last one yourself this
session.

So the surviving space is precisely characterized: **any successful method must use what makes
the pair `{x, x+2}` degenerate — its two linear parts are proportional — and not merely its
local densities.** That is a real, sharp statement. Now turn it into a plan of attack.

## What I need from you — a plan, not a proof

Choose one path into the surviving space. Do not choose a dead class, and do not restate the
barriers. Give me:

1. **The object.** The one mathematical object or identity that the method will control — stated
   exactly, with its definition.
2. **The first move.** The single lemma or estimate that, if proved, would be real progress and
   not a restatement. State it with ranges and exponents where they apply.
3. **The chain.** The three-to-five steps from that first lemma to a twin-prime result (or a
   sharp unconditional bound beyond Chen). One line each.
4. **The wall.** The one step in that chain that is hardest, and *why* it is hard — a mechanism,
   not "nobody knows how."
5. **The verdict.** Is this attackable now (and by whom: you, a human, Lean), or does it reduce
   to a named open problem — and if so, which one, stated precisely.

## Constraints

- Do not use or name a method from the three dead classes except to say why yours is different.
- State in one line which class your method belongs to and why it is outside the three.
- No graceful "here is where it fails" exit — if a step is blocked, name the block precisely and
  the sharpest unconditional statement that survives.
- Lean-ready for any fragment you can give; exact definitions and proof obligations for the rest.

Be bold. Be specific. A plan is worth more than a gesture.
