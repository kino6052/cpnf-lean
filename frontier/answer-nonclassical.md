**Decision: yes.** There is a process arithmetic in which a single total step emits two new, permanently certified primes of the form \(6n-1,6n+1\), then returns to the same interface. It does not use a weaker test for primality. It changes what can be generated as a number.

The departure is this: **iteration has two constructors, not one**. Besides adding a tally, one can shift an entire finite tally record into the next column. Shifting is not an abbreviation for adding some fixed number of tallies. This gives a counterexample, in the shared arithmetic vocabulary, to a theorem of ordinary arithmetic. It also gives the twin loop its witnesses.

## (a) The logic and the generator

A number certificate is a finite row of tally columns
\[
[a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_d].
\]
Each \(a_i\) is a finite unary word, with length also written \(a_i\). Trailing empty columns are erased; the empty row is zero. There are two instructions on rows:

* **tick** adds one tally in column zero;
* **shift** inserts an empty column at the low end, then normalizes.

These instructions, starting from zero, generate every row: build it from its highest column down, shifting once between columns. Addition merges columns by tally addition. In multiplication, each tally in column \(i\) of the first input and each tally in column \(j\) of the second contribute one tally in column \(i+j\) of the output. In particular, multiplication is distributive. Write \(1=\mathrm{tick}(0)\) and \(X=\mathrm{shift}(1)\).

There is also **one deterministic enumerating generator**; no choice of construction history is part of numerical equality. Emit zero first. For a nonzero row define its weight by
\[
w(a)=d+\sum_i a_i.
\]
Enumerate by increasing weight, breaking ties lexicographically on the column lists from the low end. A shell of weight \(h\) can be generated by trying lengths at most \(h+1\) and entries at most \(h\), and checking normalization and weight. Thus each row has exactly one birth stage and a finite, replayable certificate for that stage. Rows are the compact notation for these certified stages. Arithmetic on stages means computing the output row and generating through its birth. **Chronological next** and **add one** are different instructions.

Use intuitionistic natural deduction with equality, \(\bot,\land,\lor,\to,\exists,\forall\) on generated data. Its judgments are \(\Gamma\vdash t:A\) for constructions and \(\Gamma\vdash e:P\) for evidence. The data grammar needs finite words, finite lists, products and their structurally recursive programs; use induction on words and lists. Numerical quantifiers take row certificates as inputs or return them as witnesses. There is no object of *all* birth stages, power set of stages, or completed run in this grammar. The unary words used for counters are data, not a second domain over which the word “prime” ranges.

This is **not a conservative extension of ordinary arithmetic**. In fact it is a replacement, with incompatible theorems in the shared \(0,1,+,=\) vocabulary. It proves
\[
\exists z\,\bigl(z\ne0\ \land\ \neg\exists y\ (z=y+1)\bigr).
\]
Take \(z=X=[0,1]\): its low column is empty, whereas that of \(y+1\) is not. Ordinary arithmetic proves the negation. This difference does not come from merely suppressing excluded middle, and it does not mention primes.

## (b) Survivor, stagnation and step identity

**Sieve.** At each birth other than \(0,1\), test the newborn \(a\) against the list of earlier survivors. Reject it if some listed \(b\) divides \(a\), meaning that \(bc=a\) for a row \(c\); otherwise emit a prime certificate and append \(a\) to the list. The certificate is the birth record and the replayable negative tests. A divisor test on nonzero inputs is finite: if \(bc=a\), both the degree (highest occupied column) and total tally count of \(c\) are bounded by those of \(a\). This follows because degrees add and total counts multiply on nonzero rows.

**Why this sieve is exact.** If \(a=bc\) and both factors differ from \(0,1\), each has strictly smaller weight than \(a\). Indeed, multiplying \(b\) by \(c\) either increases degree, or \(c\) is a constant with at least two tallies and multiplication increases total count. The symmetric argument applies to \(c\). Consequently every reducible newborn has an earlier irreducible divisor, by descent in weight. Every irreducible newborn escapes every earlier nonunit divisor: such a divisor could only equal the newborn itself. Induction along the enumeration now identifies emitted primes exactly with nonzero nonunits admitting no factorization into two nonunits. This is a proof about the sieve, not its definition.

**Loop rule.** Let a cursor be a finite prefix of the deterministic birth-and-sieve trace, including the empty prefix. Write \(s\prec t\) for proper prefix extension. For an event predicate \(E(s,t)\) on a finite extension, use the rule
\[
\frac{\displaystyle
  \Gamma\vdash u:I(s_0)
  \qquad
  \Gamma,s,i:I(s)\vdash
     f(s,i):\sum_{t}\bigl((s\prec t)\land E(s,t)\land I(t)\bigr)}
 {\displaystyle
  \Gamma\vdash\mathrm{unfold}(u,f):\mathrm{Loop}_E(s_0)} .
\]
Here \(s\) in the second premise is an arbitrary cursor, and the sum returns an actual cursor with evidence. The elimination instruction `pull` returns one such eventful extension and a suspended loop at its endpoint. Its computation rule on `unfold` is to execute \(f\) once and repackage the returned invariant evidence with **the same \(f\)**. This is a guarded coalgebra: identity of step means identity of the transition program and invariant family, not equality of successive states. In particular, a step returning only “search again” cannot inhabit the premise.

For twins, \(E(s,t)\) means: **in the part of the trace strictly after \(s\) and through \(t\), two prime births occur**, with rows \(p,q\), and the extension supplies a row \(n\) satisfying
\[
p+1=6n,\qquad q=6n+1.
\]
Here \(6\) is six ticks, and the first equation avoids needing total subtraction. In particular \(q=p+2\). The gap is arithmetic, not a claim that the two birth timestamps differ by two.

Permanent stagnation at \(s\) is the formula
\[
\mathrm{Stag}(s):=\forall t\ (s\prec t\to\neg E(s,t)).
\]
It is fully statable. So is the negation of loop existence.

## (c) The twin theorem

**Theorem.** From an arbitrary cursor \(s\) one can construct \(\mathrm{Loop}_{E}(s)\). In particular no cursor is permanently stagnant.

The finite calculation that pays for each unfold is the following. Write a row in column notation as a polynomial, so that \(uX+v\) means \(v\) low tallies and \(u\) tallies in column one.

**Two-column lemma.** If \(u>0\) and no ordinary tally count greater than one divides both \(u\) and \(v\), then \(uX+v\) is a sieve prime.

Indeed, in any factorization of this row the degrees sum to one. One factor must be a positive constant \(c\). The multiplication instruction then says \(c\) divides both column counts. Hence \(c=1\). Exactness of the sieve gives the prime birth. This proof uses no statement about prime pairs, even finite ones.

Now implement the step. Read the last weight \(H\) at the cursor, taking \(H=0\) for the empty cursor or zero birth. Set \(k=H+1\) as a tally count. Construct
\[
n=X+k,\qquad p=6X+(6k-1),\qquad q=6X+(6k+1).
\]
The low counts exist, the two twin equations hold column by column, and
\[
\gcd(6,6k-1)=\gcd(6,6k+1)=1
\]
because a common divisor divides the difference from \(6k\), namely one. The two-column lemma proves both prime births. Their weights are \(6k+6\) and \(6k+8\), respectively, both strictly beyond \(H\).

Run the generator through the shell of weight \(6k+8\), retain the two birth certificates, and return the resulting cursor. This is a bounded computation, including all intervening sieve tests. The invariant \(I\) is just “valid cursor”; trace extension preserves it. The loop rule applies. A single `pull` already refutes \(\mathrm{Stag}(s)\). ∎

**Where the work is.** Shift creates a number not obtainable by ticking zero any finite number of times. That is the substantive ontological choice. It does *not* stipulate that shifted numbers or their neighbors are prime. For instance \(6X+8=2(3X+4)\) is rejected. Nor can the loop rule make arbitrary prime patterns productive: the same sieve can never emit a pair \(6n,6n+2\) for \(n\ne0,1\), since its first member has the displayed nonunit factorization \(6\cdot n\). What distinguishes the successful pattern is the degree-one factor bound **together with** the unit differences in the low column.

## (d) Model and soundness

Use a finite-trace operational model. Data are normalized finite words and lists; a cursor is accepted by replay from the initial machine. A ground equality is realized by equal normal forms, conjunction by pairs, disjunction by tagged alternatives, existence by a witness with evidence, and implication and universality by programs taking realizers or generated inputs to realizers. No program realizes \(\bot\). Structural recursion and induction are sound by descent on the finite input.

For the loop judgment, a semantic realizer is a machine with a `pull` operation such that every finite sequence of pull requests succeeds, returns valid consecutive eventful extensions, and leaves a machine of the same kind. The loop rule is sound by induction on a supplied finite request word: its step premise terminates, validates the next output, and restores the invariant needed for the remaining requests. **Forever is licensed by this uniform restoration**, not by a collection of unrelated witnesses for different requested lengths. There is no instruction to request a completed infinite output. Since the empty row and the one-tally row have different normal forms, soundness excludes, for example, a proof of \(0=1\).

There is a useful external description of the data model: it is the semiring \(\mathbb N[X]\) of polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients. The sieve primes here are *multiplicative irreducibles*; no assumption equating irreducibles and prime elements in arbitrary semirings is needed. The classical description makes the alteration transparent. A classical model of a process logic does not make that logic an extension of classical **integer arithmetic**.

## (e) Checker

A checker can be built as a shallow embedding in **Lean 4**, with no new coinduction axiom. The minimal implementation is:

1. Encode columns by `List Unit` or, equivalently, unary `Nat` in the host. Encode normalized rows by lists with a normalization function. Implement shell enumeration and bounded trial division.
2. Define a trace by iterating that enumerator and sieve. Prove degree addition, multiplicativity of total tally count, the weight-descent lemma, and sieve exactness. These are the generic sieve obligations.
3. Define `Event s t` by the two actual birth records and the two row equalities. Package the loop witness as an invariant family, its initial evidence, and the total step function in the displayed rule. `pull` is projection and application; a driver on a finite word of requests is structural recursion.
4. Instantiate the step with the shell bound above. The remaining obligations are the two-column lemma, the two elementary coprimality calculations, and the strict weight bounds.

Only finite-data computation, induction, and dependent function and pair checking are needed. Host `Nat` for lengths does not equip object rows with the host theorem that every nonzero row is a successor. This is an implementation specification, not a claim that the attached Lean files already check the new theorem.

## (f) Honest ledger

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Two fresh exact sieve primes \(6n-1,6n+1\) per pull | Proved for row arithmetic, without a window or provisional certificates. |
| Some nonzero number is not \(y+1\) for any \(y\) | Proved here; **false** in classical natural-number arithmetic. |
| Restriction to one-column numbers | Multiplication and factorization there are ordinary: a nonzero constant cannot acquire a positive-degree factor. The loop does **not** produce witnesses in this restriction. |
| Xue’s `OftenDirect`, or unbounded classical integer twins | Not decided by this argument. About classical twin-prime infinitude the decision implies **nothing**. |

The change is in the generator, not in the standard of evidence. If shifting a tally into a new column had to stand for multiplication by a fixed ordinary base, the two-column lemma would fail: evaluating \(6X+5\) at \(X=5\), for example, gives \(35\). Keeping the column as generated structure, rather than evaluating it away, is exactly what makes the productive step sound.
