  A Computational Framework for Prime Numbers:
A Case Study on How Reification and Ontology Affect
           Prime Infinitude Arguments
                                         Yong Xue

                 Dedicated to Li Juan, another mystery yet to be solved.

                                          Abstract
         This paper examines how reification—the treatment of mathematical processes
     as completed objects—can introduce unnecessary obstacles into mathematical rea-
     soning. We introduce the Computational Prime Number Framework (CPNF),
     a formal model in which primality is treated not as an inherent property of integers
     but as the outcome of a stagewise certification process. Using CPNF as a case
     study, we show how shifting from a reified ontology of “completed primes” to a
     process-oriented ontology reframes questions of infinitude and pattern recurrence.
     Within this framework, we demonstrate that the twin-prime certification process
     cannot remain permanently stagnant—i.e., it cannot reach a state after which no
     new certifications ever occur. This result relies on elementary modular arithmetic
     and a fixed-modulus reduction that becomes natural within CPNF’s stage-relative
     semantics. The paper does not claim to resolve classical conjectures but rather illus-
     trates how ontological reframing can alter the landscape of mathematical reasoning,
     revealing how reification may heighten perceived difficulty.
         Keywords: Philosophy of mathematics, reification, computational number the-
     ory, prime numbers, ontology, constructive mathematics, process philosophy

Important Notice. This paper presents a conceptual case study within a formally
defined framework. It makes no claims about the truth or falsity of classical number-
theoretic conjectures. The arguments offered are valid relative to the definitions, axioms,
and procedures of CPNF.
Methodological Note. The paper is primarily philosophical in aim: it investigates
how reification—the conversion of dynamic processes into static objects—influences proof
design and perceived difficulty. CPNF is offered as an explicit, self-contained model in
which certain classical obstacles (e.g., the parity problem) do not arise because they

philosophical point, not to serve as a complete formal proof in classical number theory.

1    Introduction: The Problem of Reification in Math-
     ematics
Mathematical practice is often guided by implicit ontological commitments—assumptions
about what mathematical objects are and how they exist. One particularly pervasive
commitment is reification: the treatment of ongoing processes, dynamic constructions,
or temporal verifications as if they were completed, static objects. This tendency, criti-
cized by philosophers such as Henri Bergson [1], Alfred North Whitehead [15], and more
recently by process philosophers and some strands of constructive mathematics (e.g.,
Bishop [2]; Martin-Löf [11]), can obscure the procedural nature of mathematical discov-
ery and introduce artificial barriers in reasoning.
    In number theory, primes are typically conceived as pre-existing objects with fixed
properties. This reified view leads naturally to questions about the “set of all primes”
or the “distribution of twin primes” as completed infinities—questions that are then
approached with tools designed to measure or count such objects. But what if primes are
more naturally understood as certifications issued by an ongoing process of verification?
This paper explores that alternative.
    We introduce the Computational Prime Number Framework (CPNF), a model
that treats primality not as a static property but as a status conferred by a stepwise,
verifiable filtering process. In CPNF, primes emerge as survivors of a deterministic sieve
that proceeds in discrete stages, each stage certifying one new number as “prime relative
to current knowledge.” This process-oriented ontology echoes constructive tendencies in
Euclid (c. 300 BCE) and Eratosthenes (c. 200 BCE) but also aligns with philosophical
traditions that resist reification [1, 15].
    Our goal is not to prove new theorems in classical number theory but to demonstrate
how a shift away from reification can reshape mathematical reasoning. We focus on twin
primes—pairs of the form (6n − 1, 6n + 1)—and show that within CPNF, the certifi-

                                            3
cation process for such pairs cannot enter permanent stagnation. The argument relies
on elementary modular arithmetic and a fixed-modulus reduction, which becomes natural
within the stage-relative certification semantics of CPNF.
    We proceed as follows:

    • §2 defines CPNF for ordinary primes and shows how a classical infinitude argument
      is re-expressed as a non-termination property.

    • §3 extends CPNF to twin-prime patterns.

    • §4 identifies the central conceptual hurdle—the endless chase scenario—and resolves
      it via fixed-modulus reduction, explaining why adversarial covering is not modeled
      in CPNF.

    • §5 reflects on how this approach circumvents classical obstacles like the parity prob-
      lem by avoiding reification.

    • §6 discusses the philosophical implications, engaging with contemporary work on
      ontology and process in mathematics.

This study suggests that certain long-standing barriers in number theory may be, in part,
artifacts of reification—and that alternative, process-oriented perspectives can open up
distinct, and sometimes simpler, forms of reasoning.


2     CPNF: A Process-Oriented Ontology for Primality
2.1     Basic Framework
Let N+ denote the positive integers. In CPNF, primality is not defined via divisibility
alone but as the outcome of a certification process. The core components are:

    • Blocker set Bk : A finite set of numbers certified by stage k. Initially B0 = ∅.

    • Candidate set: All integers ≥ 2.

    • Sieve operator:

                        S(Bk , N+ ) = {n ∈ N+ : n ≥ 2 ∧ ∀p ∈ Bk , p ∤ n}.


The process proceeds in stages (see Appendix A for formal definitions and algorithms):

    1. Initialization: B0 = ∅.

    2. Stage k:


                                             4
         • Compute survivors S(Bk−1 , N+ ).
         • Select the minimal survivor nk .
         • Certify nk by setting Bk = Bk−1 ∪ {nk }.

Thus, each certified number comes with a finite certificate: the list of blockers it survived.
   For example, the first stage certifies 2 (minimal integer ≥ 2 with no blockers). The
second certifies 3 (minimal survivor not divisible by 2), and so on.


2.2    Properties of the Process
Soundness: No composite number is ever certified (Theorem A.1.4 in Appendix A).
Non-termination: The process never halts (Theorem A.1.5 in Appendix A).
   This reformulates Euclid’s classical argument [5] as the non-termination of a procedure
rather than a statement about an infinite set, aligning with constructive mathematics [2].

4     The Endless Chase and Fixed-Modulus Reduction
4.1     The Endless Chase Scenario
Could the process reach a state after which no new twin index is ever certified, even as
Bk grows? This is the endless chase: a hypothetical where all potential twin indices
forever lie beyond the certification window Wk .


4.2     Why Density Heuristics Are Insufficient
Classical density estimates suggest the number of admissible indices up to Wk grows
roughly like yk2 /(log yk )2 [7]. However, since the modulus Mk = p∈Bk p grows super-
                                                                      Q

exponentially, Wk is shorter than a full period of the sieve lattice. Thus, average density
does not guarantee existence in short intervals.


4.3     Fixed-Modulus Reduction as an Analytic Lens
CPNF’s stage-relative semantics enables a simplification: To analyze progress beyond

stage k0 , freeze the modulus at M0 = p∈Bk p and work within the fixed periodic lattice

of residues modulo M0 . This reduction is an analytic tool, not altering the procedure.
    Intuitively, imagine the residues modulo M0 as a repeating grid. Admissible residues
R0 (Definition C.1 in Appendix C) form a non-empty set of “safe” positions for twin
indices. Each new prime q > max(Bk0 ) eliminates at most two classes modulo q, lifting
to at most 2M0 /q eliminations modulo M0 (Lemma C.2 in Appendix C). As yk grows,
eliminations accumulate logarithmically, while Wk ∼ yk2 /6 grows quadratically.
    For example, suppose k0 is such that Bk0 = {2, 3, 5}, M0 = 30. Then R0 excludes
residues where 6r ≡ ±1 (mod p) for p = 2, 3, 5. New primes like 7 eliminate specific
patterns in this grid, but slowly.




                                            6
4.4     Why Permanent Stagnation Cannot Occur
The density of admissible residues decays at most as 1/(log yk )2 (Lemma C.3 in Ap-
pendix C, using Mertens’ theorem [12]). Combined with quadratic window growth, for
large yk , Wk > M0 , spanning multiple periods. Cumulative eliminations grow too slowly
to exhaust R0 without forming a covering system over R0 .
    In CPNF, blockers are generated deterministically without foresight, excluding ad-
versarial alignments that could engineer a covering (Appendix D). Thus, no finite set of
subsequent primes can cover all of R0 , preventing permanent stagnation. This is shown
via reduction: Stagnation implies a finite covering (Theorem D.1), but such coverings are
not realized in CPNF’s procedural setup (Lemma D.2 provides a bound).
    This argument uses only growth rates and periodicity, not uniform distribution.


5     Why This Shift Matters: Ontology, Reification,
      and Proof Strategy
5.1     Reification and the Parity Problem
The parity problem—inability of sieve methods to distinguish even vs. odd numbers of
prime factors—arises from reifying sieves as tools for measuring completed sets [14]. In
CPNF, the sieve constructs an ongoing process, making parity irrelevant.
   Why parity doesn’t apply:

    1. Stage-relative certifications: No absolute primality claims.

    2. Process over properties: Focus on certification machine behavior.

    3. Different criteria: Success is non-stagnation, not counting.


5.2     The Dangers of Reification
Reification [1, 15] mistakes dynamic processes for static objects, leading to:

    • Unnecessary complexity: Framing in completed infinities requires advanced
      tools.

    • Hypothetical obstacles: Adversarial coverings excluded in CPNF.

    • Epistemic opacity: Obscuring constructive origins.

CPNF resists this by foregrounding the process, changing valid proofs and relevant ob-
stacles (cf. constructive approaches in Martin-Löf [11]).

6     Discussion and Philosophical Reflections
CPNF illustrates how ontological choices—reified vs. process-oriented—reshape practice.
Process ontology simplifies strategies and avoids obstacles.
   This reframes problems without “solving” them classically, showing difficulty depends
on framework [6, 10].


6.1    Engagement with Contemporary Philosophy
Aligns with:

    • Shapiro [13] on mathematical structures.

    • Bueno [3] on representations.

    • Ladyman [8] on structural realism and process.

    • Burgess [4] on inference reliability.

Resonates with modal infinitude [9] treating infinities as potential.
    Critically, CPNF differs from strict intuitionism by being framework-relative, not
rejecting classical logic outright.


6.2    Limitations and Future Directions
CPNF is simplified; value in explicitness. Future: Apply to other domains (analysis,
geometry); relate to type theory [11]; explore epistemic implications of certifications vs.
discoveries.


7     Conclusion
This paper presents CPNF as a philosophical case study on reification’s dangers and
process ontologies’ potential. Reconceiving primes as stagewise certifications reframes
twin-prime infinitude as non-stagnation. Fixed-modulus reduction, natural in CPNF,
resolves endless chase elementary, excluding adversarial coverings by construction.
    Within CPNF, twin-pattern recurrence is established without parity barriers or dis-
tributional hypotheses. Methodologically, reflecting on ontological commitments resists
reification, enriching practice and understanding.
    CPNF’s novelty: Explicit process ontology disentangling infinitude, obstruction, and
certification.

Theorem 3 (Soundness). No composite integer is ever selected by the ordinary CPNF
process.
Proof. Suppose a composite number m were selected at some stage. Let p be its smallest
prime divisor. Since p < m and p divides m, p would have been selected earlier (as the
minimal survivor not divisible by any previous blocker). Then p ∈ B would block m,
contradicting m’s selection as a survivor.
Theorem 4 (Non-termination). The CPNF process for ordinary primes never termi-
nates.
Proof. Suppose only finitely many survivors p1 , . . . , pm were certified. Consider M =
p1 p2 · · · pm +1. This number is not divisible by any pi , so M ∈ S({p1 , . . . , pm }, N+ ). Since
M > max(pi ), it (or a smaller candidate) would be selected as the next minimal survivor,
contradicting the assumption of finiteness. This mirrors Euclid’s classical proof [5].

E     Philosophical Remark
Within CPNF, primes are not assumed as pre-existing objects but arise as certifications
in a deterministic process. This process-oriented ontology shifts the twin prime question
from one of asymptotic distribution to one of structural obstruction: stagnation would
require a finite set of primes to eliminate all admissible configurations generated by earlier
stages.
    The appendices make no probabilistic assumptions and do not purport to resolve
the twin prime conjecture. Their role is to demonstrate how ontological commitments
alter the form of the mathematical problem—transforming an analytic question into a
combinatorial one—thereby supporting the philosophical thesis of the paper.
